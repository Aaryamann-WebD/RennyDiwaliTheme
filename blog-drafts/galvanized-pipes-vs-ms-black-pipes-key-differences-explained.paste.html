What Are MS Black Pipes?

MS Black Pipes are uncoated mild steel pipes, usually made by electric resistance welding (ERW). Hot-rolled coil is slit, formed into a tube and welded along the seam with high-frequency current. The dark, matte finish comes from mill scale, an iron oxide layer formed during hot rolling.

Mild Steel Pipe Properties That Matter in Service

  • Strength: the full mechanical strength of the base steel
  • Weldability: no coating to burn off, so site welding is fast and clean
  • Heat tolerance: stable at high temperatures, which suits steam and process lines
  • Cost: the lowest upfront price of any steel pipe option

These properties belong to the base steel, so galvanized pipe shares them. Zinc adds protection, not strength.

Two finishes are sold in the market, and they behave differently on site.

Varnish gives better corrosion resistance than bare scale, a cleaner look, less contamination in storage and less preparation before painting. It is still not galvanizing: a varnished pipe has no zinc layer, no sacrificial protection and is not meant for long-term outdoor or water-carrying service. What Are Galvanized Steel Pipes?

Galvanized steel pipes are steel pipes, usually MS Black Pipes, coated in zinc. In India they are widely called GI pipes, short for galvanized iron, although the base metal is mild steel. They are easy to spot by their silvery-grey surface, which may be spangled or matte.

How Hot-Dip Galvanizing Builds the Zinc Coating

  1. Clean the pipe to remove oil and dirt.
  2. Pickle it in acid to strip rust and scale.
  3. Flux it so the zinc wets the surface evenly.
  4. Dip it in molten zinc at around 450°C, where the zinc bonds metallurgically with the steel, inside and out.

Barrier and Sacrificial Protection

  • Barrier: the zinc layer seals steel off from moisture and oxygen.
  • Sacrificial (cathodic): zinc corrodes before steel, so scratches and cut edges stay protected.

GI Pipe vs ERW Pipe: Clearing Up the Confusion

"GI pipe vs ERW pipe" is one of the most searched comparisons in Indian steel procurement, and one of the most misunderstood. ERW describes how a pipe is made. GI describes how it is finished. Most GI pipes sold in India are ERW pipes that were hot-dip galvanized after welding.

The real comparison is between two finishes of the same pipe: MS Black Pipe (ERW, uncoated) and galvanized pipe (ERW with zinc). Seamless pipes, pierced from a solid billet, are a separate category used mainly for high-pressure oil, gas and boiler duty.

Difference Between Galvanized and Black Pipes: What Actually Changes

The table above gives the verdicts. The reasons behind them are below.

Zinc Coating Thickness: Reading GSM, Microns and IS 4736

IS 4736 requires a minimum zinc coating mass of 360 g/m², measured across inner and outer surfaces. Renny's galvanized pipes carry 385 g/m² (385 GSM). Zinc weighs about 7.14 g/m² for every micron of thickness, so mass converts to thickness like this:

Coating massApproximate thicknessNote
360 g/m²About 50 micronsIS 4736 minimum
385 g/m²About 54 micronsRenny galvanized pipes

This matters when comparing quotes. Some suppliers quote grams per square metre and others quote microns, and standards such as EN ISO 1461 specify thickness on a different basis. Ask which measure a figure uses before treating two numbers as equal. Uniformity and adhesion are checked by standard tests, including a bend test for smaller bores.

Corrosion Resistance: How Each Pipe Fails

  • MS Black Pipe: red rust forms once the mill scale or varnish is breached, followed by pitting. In water lines, rust scale also builds up inside the bore and restricts flow.
  • Galvanized pipe: the zinc wears away slowly instead of the steel. White rust, a powdery zinc corrosion product, appears in damp or poorly ventilated storage and is the early warning sign.
  • Limits: galvanizing is not corrosion-proof. Strong acids and some chemicals attack zinc, so aggressive process fluids need a different specification.

Steel Pipe Durability: Estimated Zinc Life by Environment

Durability depends on how fast the environment consumes the zinc. ISO 9223 grades atmospheres from C2 to C5 and gives a zinc loss range for each. Dividing a 54-micron coating by those rates gives an estimate of how long the coating lasts on outer surfaces:

Environment (ISO 9223)Zinc loss, microns per yearEstimated coating life
C2: dry, rural, sheltered0.1 to 0.775+ years
C3: urban, light industry0.7 to 2.1About 26 to 77 years
C4: industrial, inland coastal2.1 to 4.2About 13 to 26 years
C5: marine, heavy industry4.2 to 8.4About 6 to 13 years

These are estimates, not guarantees. They explain why 20 to 50 years is realistic for typical outdoor use and why coastal projects need heavier coating or added protection. Buried pipe depends on soil chemistry, and internal life depends on water quality.

Welding and Fabricating Each Pipe

  • MS Black Pipe: weld directly; there is nothing to burn off.
  • Galvanized pipe: heating zinc releases zinc oxide fumes. Use extraction and protective equipment, then restore the weld zone with zinc-rich paint.
  • Cutting and threading: both remove zinc at the cut. Touch up every cut end and thread on galvanized pipe.

Gas, Heat and Pressure Limits

In gas systems, flakes of zinc from the inner wall can block regulators and burner orifices, which is why MS Black Pipes remain the accepted choice for natural gas and LPG. Galvanized coating also degrades above about 200°C, so steam, boiler and hot-oil lines use MS Black Pipe. Pressure rating, by contrast, comes from wall thickness and steel grade, not from the zinc.

Upfront vs Lifecycle Cost

Galvanized pipes cost more at purchase, but the full cost includes upkeep.

Lifecycle cost = purchase price + (repaint cost x number of repaints) + replacement cost, if life is shorter than the horizon

An illustrative index over 20 years, not a price list: MS Black Pipe at 100, galvanized at 120 (a 20% premium). If the MS Black Pipe needs four repaints at 20 each, its lifecycle cost is 180 against 120 for galvanized. In dry, sheltered indoor service with no repainting, MS Black Pipe stays cheaper. In humid, coastal or buried service, the premium is usually recovered.

Galvanized Steel Pipe Specifications and Standards

In India, the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) sets galvanized steel pipe specifications through three documents:

  • IS 1239 (Part 1): mild steel tubes for water, gas, air and steam, from 15 NB to 150 NB
  • IS 4736: hot-dip zinc coating on mild steel tubes
  • IS 3589: larger-diameter steel pipes for water and sewage

Steel Pipe Applications: Where Each Pipe Belongs

The right pipe depends on what flows through it and where it is installed.

Water Supply and Building Plumbing

Galvanized pipes are used for potable water, building distribution, agricultural irrigation, borewell columns and rural drinking water schemes. Public infrastructure drives demand: under the Jal Jeevan Mission, rural tap water coverage rose from 17% of households in 2019 to about 79.7% by February 2025, and the mission has been extended to 2028.

Gas, Steam and Process Lines

MS Black Pipes serve natural gas and LPG distribution, steam, boiler and hot-oil lines, and any line that will be welded and painted on site.

Fire Protection

Split the system by function. Fire sprinkler lines typically use galvanized pipes. Fire hydrant mains use MS Black Pipes with internal and external protection.

Structural, Fencing and Solar

  • MS Black Pipes: structural frames, columns and trusses, plus mechanical, automotive and general engineering parts.
  • Galvanized pipes: scaffolding, fencing, railings and handrails, greenhouse frames, solar module mounting structures, and electrical and telecom conduit.

Steel Pipe Maintenance: Galvanized vs MS Black Pipes

Maintenance is where the two pipes differ most after installation.

Maintaining MS Black Pipes

  • Prime and paint exposed pipe promptly, and repaint on a cycle set by the environment.
  • Store pipe off the ground and under cover to limit rust before use.
  • Touch up varnish scratches, since varnish is only a thin film.
  • In water service, watch for internal scale and flow loss.

Maintaining Galvanized Steel Pipes

  • Routine upkeep is light: the zinc does the work.
  • Inspect cut ends, threads and weld zones, and touch up with zinc-rich paint.
  • Treat white rust early; keep stacks ventilated and dry.

Inspection Checklist and Replacement Signs

  • Rust or flaking across a growing share of an MS Black Pipe's surface
  • Continuous white rust or bulging on a galvanized pipe
  • Rusty or discoloured water at outlets
  • Falling flow or pressure
  • Leaks at threads or joints
  • Bare steel at cuts or welds that was never touched up

How to Choose Between Galvanized Pipes and MS Black Pipes

Five questions settle most specifications:

  1. What will flow through it? Water points to galvanized. Gas, steam or oil points to MS Black Pipe.
  2. Where will it be installed? Outdoor, buried, humid or coastal exposure favours galvanized.
  3. Will it be welded on site? MS Black Pipe is simpler; galvanized needs fume control and touch-up.
  4. What is the operating temperature? Above about 200°C, choose MS Black Pipe.
  5. Which cost matters more? Lowest purchase price: MS Black Pipe. Lowest cost over the pipe's life: usually galvanized.

Common Buying and Installation Mistakes

  1. Specifying galvanized pipe for gas lines. Zinc flakes cause the blockages described above.
  2. Treating varnish as galvanizing. A varnished MS Black Pipe has no zinc layer.
  3. Skipping the coating check. Read the coating mass on the mill test certificate, not just the product name.
  4. Joining dissimilar metals without isolation. Zinc and steel together are generally fine, because the zinc keeps protecting. The risk is copper, brass or stainless components in the same line, so use dielectric fittings.
  5. Buying pre-galvanized (GP) pipe for buried water. Its uncoated weld seam is the weak point underground; specify hot-dip galvanized.

How to Verify a Pipe Is Really Galvanized

  1. Read the mill test certificate and compare coating mass with the IS 4736 minimum.
  2. Measure thickness on a sample with a magnetic gauge.
  3. Ask for a coating uniformity test (IS 2633).
  4. Inspect the surface: true zinc is continuous and bonded, while silver paint over black steel scratches off.

Frequently Asked Questions

What Is the Difference Between Galvanized Pipes and MS Black Pipes?

Galvanized pipes carry a hot-dip zinc coating that resists rust, so they suit water and outdoor use. MS Black Pipes are uncoated mild steel, so they suit gas, steam and welded structures, and cost less upfront.

Is GI Pipe the Same as ERW Pipe?

Not exactly. ERW is the manufacturing method; GI is the zinc finish. A GI pipe is usually an ERW pipe that was hot-dip galvanized after welding.

Can Galvanized Pipes Be Used for Gas Lines?

Not recommended. Zinc can flake from the inner wall and block regulators and burner orifices. MS Black Pipes are the standard for natural gas and LPG.

Can Black Steel Pipe Be Used for Drinking Water?

Not uncoated. Bare mill-scale steel rusts in water, which hurts flow and water quality. Use galvanized pipes, or suitably protected pipe, for potable supply.

How Long Do Galvanized Pipes Last?

Typically 20 to 50 years in outdoor and water-supply use. Life depends on coating thickness, water chemistry, soil conditions and installation quality.

What Is the Minimum Zinc Coating on GI Pipes in India?

IS 4736 sets a minimum of 360 g/m² for hot-dip galvanized mild steel tubes, measured across inner and outer surfaces. Renny's galvanized pipes carry 385 g/m².

Can Galvanized Pipes Be Welded?

Yes, but heating zinc releases zinc oxide fumes. Use extraction and protective equipment, then touch up the weld zone with zinc-rich paint.

How Much More Do Galvanized Pipes Cost Than MS Black Pipes?

Typically 15 to 25% more upfront. Over about 20 years, galvanized pipes often cost less in humid, coastal or buried service because they avoid repainting and early replacement.

Which Is Stronger, MS Black Pipe or Galvanized Pipe?

Both are equally strong, because both start as the same mild steel pipe. Zinc adds corrosion protection, not mechanical strength.

How Do I Tell a Galvanized Pipe From an MS Black Pipe?

Galvanized pipe has a silvery-grey zinc surface; MS Black Pipe is dark and matte. For proof, check the coating mass on the mill test certificate.
